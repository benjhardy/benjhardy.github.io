Why this order works
  • Predict before you look. Committing to a guess, then seeing the sim, then explaining the gap is the predict, observe, explain loop from physics teaching. A wrong guess you had to own sticks better than a right answer you read.
  • Test yourself instead of rereading. Retrieval practice beats restudying for long-term recall (Roediger and Karpicke, 2006). Dunlosky et al. (2013) rated practice testing and spaced practice the two most useful study techniques.
  • Mix the topics. The drills interleave Γ, dB, reactance, Q and load-line problems. Mixing forces you to pick the right formula, which is what the interview asks of you (Rohrer and Taylor, 2007).
  • Learn anchor numbers. A few memorized points (Γ 0.2 is 14 dB, 1 pF at 10 GHz is 16 Ω) let you estimate everything else. That speed is what an interviewer reads as fluency.
  • Explain out loud. Each tab ends with the sentence to say in the room. Saying it without looking is self-explanation, and it shows you where your understanding has gaps.
